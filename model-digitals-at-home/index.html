How to take model digitals at home

By Drew NerosModel since 2015, photographer

You don't need a studio or a photographer. You need a plain wall, a window and about twenty minutes. Agencies use digitals to rule people out, so the goal is to give them nothing to rule out.

Digitals are not a photoshoot

A good photograph and a good digital are different things. If your digitals look styled, lit or retouched, the agent assumes you are hiding something and moves on. Keep it plain: even light, a plain wall, a relaxed face and skin exactly as it is.

The setup

LightStand 3 to 4 feet from a large window, turned 45° to the glass. Mid-morning or early afternoon. Overcast days are best.
WallWhite or light grey, nothing else in the frame. Stand at least 2 feet in front of it so you don't cast a shadow. A taped-up white sheet works too.
CameraRear camera, never the front one. Chest height, 5 to 6 feet away, on a tripod or a stack of books.
LensAbout 50mm equivalent. Wide angle up close distorts your face, and that alone gets you rejected.
SettingsIf you can set them: ISO 100 to 400, f/4 to f/5.6, focus locked on your eyes, highest resolution.

The six frames

  1. Front. Full length, feet together, arms relaxed.
  2. Profile. A clean 90° turn.
  3. Three-quarter, left. 45° from the front.
  4. Three-quarter, right. The same turn, mirrored.
  5. Back, smile or crop. Whatever the agency asks for. For men the back shot is optional.
  6. Close-up. Head and shoulders, eyes sharp.

Take several of each angle. Light and posture shift between frames, so pick the best ones later.

What to wear

A fitted tank or tee in one colour, no logos. Slim jeans or leggings. No makeup, or as close to none as you'll go. Hair natural, down or in a simple ponytail. No jewellery, hats or glasses. Your clothes should show your shape, not compete with it.

What gets digitals ignored

  • Retouching. Smoothed skin or slimmed features mean an instant no. Agencies need your real face.
  • Mixed light. Window light plus a lamp gives you a colour cast and hard shadows.
  • Posing. Chin up, hip out, dramatic angles. You are documenting, not performing.
  • A busy background. Furniture and patterned walls pull focus.
  • The wrong lens. Step back and zoom in rather than shooting wide up close.

One more thing on expression. European agencies want neutral and serious. Asian markets lean softer and more approachable. Shoot for the market you are sending to.
